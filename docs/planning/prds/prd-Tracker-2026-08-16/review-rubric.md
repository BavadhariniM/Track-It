# PRD Quality Review — Tracker

## Overall verdict
This is a well-shaped, honest PRD for what it is: a lean, single-operator capability spec with unusually strong data-integrity treatment (versioning, derived status, import validation) and clean ID/glossary hygiene. The two real risks are that the product's actual differentiator — exotic custom recurrence — is specified at the enumeration level but not at the acceptance-criteria level (no anchor-date semantics for "every N days," no per-FR consequences for FR-6–FR-10), and a couple of small but concrete cross-reference slips (FR-4 → FR-30, addendum's dangling "NFR-3") that a downstream builder or architect would trip on. Nothing here is structurally broken; the fixes are targeted, not a rewrite.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The PRD states real decisions rather than hedging them: §5 Non-Goals draws a hard line ("No cloud sync, account system, or login — ever, by architectural design, not a deferred feature"), and §4.2's description names outright that scope was expanded mid-creation ("These requirements were explicitly expanded during PRD creation beyond what `requirements-v2.md` scoped for MVP — full exotic scheduling ships now, not deferred"). The one `[NOTE FOR PM]` in §5 sits at a genuine tension (extensibility vs. "none are built now"), not a safe checkpoint. §8 Open Questions is honestly empty at the product level and defers two real implementation questions to architecture with the invariants they must preserve stated — that's a legitimate deferral, not a dodge.

Where it falls short: the single largest scope decision in the document — overriding both `requirements-v2.md`'s explicit MVP deferral of advanced recurrence and `brief.md`'s exclusion of it — is stated confidently in both `prd.md` §4.2 and `addendum.md`'s "Superseded / Rejected Framing" section, but neither document ever names what this costs (larger engine to get right before the daily-logging loop is usable, larger test surface, more ways for FR-11's "free combination" guarantee to be wrong). The only pointer to deeper rationale is `.memlog.md`, which isn't part of the reviewed artifact set. A PM pushing back with "why take this on now instead of shipping the simple version and learning" would find the decision but not an argued cost side.

### Findings
- **medium** Cost of pulling exotic recurrence into MVP is never named (§4.2 description; addendum "Superseded / Rejected Framing") — the decision to override two prior documents' explicit deferral is asserted, not argued; the trade-off (schedule risk, test surface, correctness risk on the hardest part of the product) is absent from both `prd.md` and `addendum.md`. *Fix:* Add one or two sentences in §4.2 or the addendum naming what's given up (time-to-first-usable-version, expanded correctness surface) so the decision reads as weighed rather than asserted.

## Substance over theater — strong
No persona bloat — Target User (§2) is a single JTBD list plus one UJ, appropriately minimal for a single-operator tool rather than padded out with personas that don't drive decisions. The Vision (§1) is specific to this product's actual mechanism ("Goals carry real rules: an evaluation period, an eligible-days schedule, a tracking type, and a target comparison... That correctness *is* the product") — it could not be swapped into another tracker PRD unchanged, which is the bar. No NFR boilerplate: there is no "must be scalable/secure/reliable" language anywhere in the document (verified — zero hits for "reasonable," "gracefully," "robust," "seamless," "user-friendly" etc.); FR-18's "Data-Loss Bound" is concrete instead. No findings needed here.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is explicit and singular ("correctness *is* the product"), and feature ordering follows it: the goal engine and scheduling/target combinatorics (§4.1–4.2) come before the UI surfaces that merely display their output (§4.4–4.7). §7's Success Metrics are thesis-validating rather than activity metrics — SM-2 ("Zero instances where displayed status contradicts what the user knows actually happened, including under the exotic scheduling patterns in §4.2") is a direct test of the Vision's claim, and both SM-1 and SM-2 carry counter-metrics (SM-C1, SM-C2) rather than standing unopposed.

One coherence gap: §5 Non-Goals explicitly and permanently excludes "telemetry, analytics, or crash reporting of any kind — not even opt-in/anonymous," yet §7's SM-1 ("daily logging happens consistently rather than lapsing") and SM-3 ("Android and iOS are equally usable day-to-day") are exactly the kind of claims teams normally verify via usage analytics. With telemetry off the table by design, the PRD never states how Panda would actually know SM-1/SM-3 are true or false (periodic self-review? a stats screen glance?). For a single-user product this is answerable, but it isn't answered.

### Findings
- **medium** No stated measurement mechanism for SM-1/SM-3 given the no-telemetry non-goal (§5 vs §7) — the metrics are real and thesis-aligned, but nothing says how they'll be checked without any usage logging. *Fix:* One line per SM on how Panda self-assesses it (e.g., "reviewed monthly via the Statistics screen," §4.5's FR-26 already provides the raw data).

## Done-ness clarity — thin
This is the dimension where the PRD is least even. The daily-logging and data-management FRs are genuinely strong: FR-3 (Versioning), FR-4 (Derived Status), FR-17 (Certain-Failure Red), and FR-32 (Import) all carry specific, testable Consequences — FR-17's worked example ("A Rolling-Window '10x in any 14 days' Goal turns red the day the remaining days in the trailing 14-day window can no longer mathematically reach 10, and has no other red-triggering condition") is exactly the level of precision an engineer needs. No vague-adjective language appears anywhere in the document (checked for "reasonable," "gracefully," "intuitive," "robust," "seamless," "performant" — zero hits), which is a real strength most PRDs at this stakes level don't bother with.

But the core differentiator — §4.2's Scheduling & Target Patterns, the "exotic scheduling" the whole Vision is staked on — is thin exactly where it matters most. FR-6 through FR-10 (Evaluation Period Types, Arbitrary Selection, Custom Recurrence, Blackout Dates, Target Comparison) have **no Consequences subsections at all**; the only acceptance-level evidence for this entire combinatorial engine is FR-11's 13-row worked-example table, which is a good illustration but not a specification. Concretely: FR-8 defines "every N days" and "every N weeks (on specific weekdays)" but neither the PRD nor the addendum ever states the anchor date the N-day/N-week cycle counts from — Goal start date, last completion, something else. That's not a cosmetic gap: it changes what "eligible day" means for a pattern the PRD calls out by name as the product's edge, and it's exactly the kind of ambiguity SM-2 ("zero instances where displayed status contradicts what the user knows actually happened") is designed to catch.

Two smaller gaps worth naming: FR-24's dashboard shows "upcoming reminders" (plural) but FR-28 defines only "a single global reminder time applied across all Goals (no per-Goal custom times in MVP)" — it's unclear what a plural "reminders" widget would even render against one global time. And FR-34 (Reset/Erase All) is called out as "the only form of true, irreversible deletion in the product" but carries no Consequence requiring a confirmation step before it fires — the one action in the app with no undo is also the one FR with no safeguard stated.

### Findings
- **high** No anchor-date semantics for "every N days" / "every N weeks" recurrence (FR-8) — the PRD defines the pattern by name but never states what date the cycle counts from (Goal start date vs. last completion vs. other), leaving the product's flagship correctness claim (SM-2) unverifiable against this pattern as written. *Fix:* Add a Consequence to FR-8 stating the anchor (e.g., "the N-day/N-week cycle is anchored to the Goal's start date; edits do not re-anchor it") — this is a product decision, not purely an implementation detail, since it changes what counts as an Eligible Day.
- **high** FR-6–FR-10 (the core scheduling/target engine, §4.2) carry no per-FR testable Consequences — only FR-11's 13-row example table stands in for acceptance criteria across the entire combinatorial space the Vision is staked on. *Fix:* Add at least one Consequence per FR-6–FR-10 covering its edge behavior (e.g., FR-9: does a Blackout Date reduce the denominator for "at least N of M eligible days," or just remove that day from eligibility entirely — FR-11 row 7 implies but doesn't state which).
- **medium** FR-24 "upcoming reminders" (plural) is inconsistent with FR-28's single global reminder time (§4.5 vs §4.6) — unclear what the dashboard element actually shows. *Fix:* Either rename to singular ("next reminder time") or clarify FR-24 means something else (e.g., goals not yet logged today).
- **medium** FR-34 Reset/Erase-All has no stated confirmation-step Consequence despite being explicitly the app's only irreversible action (§4.8). *Fix:* Add a Consequence: "Reset requires an explicit secondary confirmation before executing."
- **low** FR-26 "completion percentage" computation is undefined for Goals with version history or pause gaps (§4.5) — e.g., does a paused month count against the denominator? *Fix:* One clause tying the stat to FR-2's "calendar reflects only the periods the Goal was actually active for."

## Scope honesty — strong
§5 Non-Goals does real work and is written honestly rather than deferred-vaguely — it distinguishes permanent exclusions from deferred ones explicitly ("No cloud sync... ever, by architectural design, not a deferred feature" vs. the `[NOTE FOR PM]` on percentage/conditional targets, which leaves the door open architecturally without building it). The four inline `[ASSUMPTION: ...]` tags (FR-7, FR-23, FR-28, FR-30) round-trip cleanly to §9's Assumptions Index — every inline tag is indexed, and every indexed item appears inline, 1:1. Given the agreed lean, personal-app stakes, an open-items count of 4 assumptions + 1 NOTE FOR PM + 2 architecture-deferred questions is proportionate — neither suspiciously empty nor bloated. The deliberate overrides of prior source docs (exotic recurrence pulled into MVP, timezone/DST dropped, percentage/conditional rules excluded) are captured with rationale in `addendum.md`'s "Superseded / Rejected Framing," consistent with this being an intentional, reasoned set of decisions rather than silent omissions. No findings.

## Downstream usability — adequate
Given §0 states this PRD "feeds directly into downstream UX and architecture work," this dimension carries real weight rather than being academic. The Glossary (§3) is used consistently — spot-checked "Eligible Day," "Derived Status," "Cheat Day," "Goal Version," and "Streak" against their FR usage and found no drift. FR numbering is contiguous and non-duplicated across FR-1 through FR-34. UJ-1 names its protagonist (Panda) and carries context inline rather than floating.

One concrete defect: FR-4's Consequence states "The only stored 'status' values are read-optimization caches (see FR-30), which are provably derivable from the same source data" — but FR-30 is Widget Tap-Through ("Tapping a widget opens the app to the relevant date"), which has nothing to do with caching. The caching behavior FR-4 is actually pointing at is FR-29 ("Widgets render only precomputed, cached status — never a live rule evaluation at render time"). A downstream reader following this cross-reference lands on the wrong FR.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-4's cross-reference "(see FR-30)" points to the wrong FR — the caching behavior it means to cite is FR-29, not FR-30 (§4.1 FR-4 vs. §4.7 FR-29/FR-30). *Fix:* Change "(see FR-30)" to "(see FR-29)" in FR-4's Consequences.

## Shape fit — strong
The PRD correctly reads as a single-operator capability spec, not a forced consumer-journey document. One lightweight UJ (UJ-1) is appropriate — per the rubric, UJ density would be overhead here, and the PRD avoids it. No personas beyond the single JTBD framing, no monetization/stakeholder/compliance sections — all correctly absent for a personal, offline, single-user tool, matching the explicit calibration for this project. The "slightly more rigor on data integrity" instruction is visibly honored: Goal Versioning (FR-3), Derived Status (FR-4), and Import validation (FR-32) all get real, testable specification, well above what a pure hobby throwaway would carry. No findings.

## Mechanical notes
- **Broken cross-reference (addendum → PRD):** `addendum.md`'s "Superseded / Rejected Framing" section states "The PRD (NFR-3) follows v2, not v1, on this point" (referring to the timezone/DST decision) — but `prd.md` contains no NFR section or NFR numbering anywhere (confirmed via full-text search: zero matches for "NFR," "timezone," or "DST" in `prd.md`). The citation points at an artifact that doesn't exist in the reviewed PRD. This is independent of whether the timezone/DST decision itself is adequately documented (it is, in the addendum) — it's that the addendum's pointer into the PRD is dangling.
- **Broken cross-reference (within PRD):** FR-4's "(see FR-30)" should be "(see FR-29)" — see Downstream usability finding above.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** Clean. All four inline `[ASSUMPTION: ...]` tags (FR-7, FR-23, FR-28, FR-30) are indexed in §9, and all four §9 entries have a corresponding inline tag. No orphans either direction.
- **ID continuity:** FR-1 through FR-34 are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates. SM-1/2/3 and SM-C1/C2 are contiguous. UJ-1 is the sole UJ, consistent with the single-journey shape.
- **UJ protagonist naming:** UJ-1 names "Panda" and carries her context inline throughout (no fixed time, no login, dashboard-first) — compliant with the rubric's requirement.
- **Glossary drift:** None found on spot-check. Terms ("Eligible Day," "Cheat Day," "Derived Status," "Goal Version," "Streak," "Rolling Window") are used identically in the Glossary and in the FRs that reference them.
