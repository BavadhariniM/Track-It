Getting to know Tracker

Your goals, kept honest.

Tracker is a personal goal & habit log. You decide how often something should happen — every day, a few times a week, once a month — and Tracker's calendar tells you, in one glance, whether you actually did it. This manual walks through every screen and every color you'll come across.

The four tabs

Everything in Tracker lives behind four tabs at the bottom of the screen.

☀️

Today

Every goal eligible today, in one list, plus what's still open this week and this month.

📅

Calendar

The month grid — one color per day, and the screens for browsing weeks. Tracker's default screen.

🚩

Goals

Every goal you've created, with tools to edit, pause, or archive one.

⚙️

Settings

Week start day, reminders, and backing up or restoring your data.

Creating a goal

Tap the + button from Today or Calendar. A short wizard asks five questions — none of them optional, but none of them permanent either. You can change any of it later.

  1. Name it

    Something you'll recognize at a glance — "Drink water," "Gym," "Pay rent." An optional category (like Exercise or Finance) lets you filter for it later.

  2. Pick how you'll track it

    Done / Not Done for things with no number attached (meditated, or didn't). Counter for things you count (glasses of water, push-ups, pages read).

  3. Set the rhythm

    Every day, certain days of the week, once a week, once a month — see Choosing a rhythm below for the full set of options.

  4. Set the target

    For a Counter goal: how many, and whether that's a floor to reach, a ceiling not to cross, or an exact number.

  5. Reminders & review

    Optionally set a daily nudge, then confirm — the wizard shows you a plain-English sentence describing exactly what you've set up before you save it.

Choosing a rhythm

A goal's rhythm is when it needs doing and how often the calendar checks. These are the building blocks — mix and match them freely.

Every dayMeditate — daily

Checked once per day. Miss a day and that day alone is marked missed.

Specific weekdaysGym — Mon / Wed / Fri

Only those weekdays count as eligible; every other day is simply blank.

Workdays or weekends onlyCoffee limit — workdays

Two common weekday presets, built the same way as any custom pick.

Once a weekRead — 1×/week

Checked at the end of the week (whichever day you set as your week's start).

Several times a weekWorkout — 3×/week

Do it on any three days within the week — Tracker doesn't care which three.

Once a monthPay rent — monthly

Checked at month's end; any day of the month satisfies it.

Every N days / weeksWater plants — every 3 days

Counts from the day you created the goal, not the calendar's own weeks.

A specific date each monthDeep clean — 2nd Saturday

For chores tied to a recurring date rather than a fixed weekday count.

Done, or how much

Every Counter goal also needs a comparison — it changes what "success" means for the exact same number.

ComparisonMeansExample0 logged reads as
At leastA floor to reach or beatWater — at least 8 glassesNot yet met — keep going
At mostA ceiling not to crossCoffee — at most 2Well within limit — perfectly fine
ExactlyA precise numberStairs — exactly 2 flightsShort of the mark — not yet done
💡

Why "0/2" can be green. On an at most goal, the fraction shows a ≤ before the target — like 0/≤2 — so you can tell at a glance it's a limit, not a floor. Zero used is a genuine success, not an unfinished task. An exactly goal shows = the same way.

Logging progress

You can log a goal from the Today tab, or from any date on the Calendar — logging a past day works exactly the same as logging today.

  • Done/Not Done goals — tap the row once to mark it done; tap again to undo it, exactly as if you'd never touched it.
  • Counter goals — tap the row to open a stepper: large + / − buttons for quick taps, or tap the number itself to type an exact value.
  • Long-press any row to open the Blackout Date / Cheat Day sheet for that date (see below).

The five colors

Every day, on every screen, resolves to exactly one of five states. Learn these once and the whole calendar reads at a glance.

✓
Success

Done, or your target was met.

✕
Failed

The day (or period) is over and the target genuinely wasn't met — not just "not yet."

C
Cheat day

You spent one of your planned passes on this day — see Cheat Days.

–
Not eligible

This goal simply isn't scheduled for this day (or it's a Blackout Date) — nothing to log here.

…
Still open

Today or a future day, or a week/month that hasn't closed yet — the outcome isn't decided.

⏳

Red only means "certain." Tracker never marks a day red just because you haven't hit the target yet — a "3×/week" goal stays gray all week until either you've made it mathematically impossible to reach 3, or the week is over and you fell short. Gray only turns to red once there's truly no way back.

Today vs. the whole period

This is the one idea worth sitting with, because it explains something that otherwise looks like a bug.

A goal like "Read, at least twice a week" is asking two different questions at once: did I read today? and have I read twice this week? Tracker answers both, in different places:

  • On the Today tab and in Day View, the checkmark answers the first question — it turns green the moment that specific day is logged, even if the week isn't finished yet.
  • In the weekly rollup and on the Goal Detail screen, the streak and stats answer the second question — whether the week's target was actually reached.

So it's entirely normal to mark today done and see it turn green immediately, while the goal's overall week is still shown as "in progress" elsewhere — those are two honest, different answers, not a contradiction.

Browsing weeks & months

The Calendar tab opens on the month grid — swipe left or right to move between months, and tap Jump to today to snap back.

Week View

Open it from the calendar's app bar to see one row per goal, one cell per day of the week — and swipe to browse any week, past or future, the same way you browse months.

Weeks this month

A second view, next to Week View, lays out every goal against every week of the month in one grid — so you can see at a glance which weeks were a clean success for each goal, without opening each one individually.

Blackout & Cheat Days

Long-press any day to reach both — they exist so a planned exception never counts against you, but they mean slightly different things.

Blackout Date

A day this goal simply doesn't apply — a trip, an injury, a holiday. Unlimited, and never counted as a miss. Shown as the plain "not eligible" dash, indistinguishable from a day the goal was never scheduled for.

Cheat Day

A deliberate, planned skip within a limited allowance you set when creating the goal — used up per period. Shown with its own gold "C," so you can see exactly which days you spent one.

Marking a day "Did Not Finish"

Sometimes you already know a day is a write-off before it's technically over.

Open a still-undecided day and choose Mark DNF to flag it as given up on. A small "DNF · pending period close" note appears under the goal's name — but the day's actual color doesn't change because of the flag alone: it only reflects reality once the day or period truly resolves. If another day later in the same week still saves the target, the DNF note simply disappears — it was never more than a personal note to yourself.

Streaks & stats

Tap a goal's name anywhere to open its Goal Detail page.

Current streak

Consecutive successful periods, ending at the most recent one that's actually finished.

Longest streak

Your best run ever, even if it isn't the one you're on now.

Completion %

Successful periods out of every period that's been decided — paused stretches don't count against you.

Cheat days used

A running count, so you always know how much allowance is left.

Below the numbers, a Version Timeline shows every time you've changed this goal's rules, and a Historical Calendar shows its whole life, one small square per day.

Editing, pausing, archiving

Three buttons on Goal Detail, each for a different situation.

  • Edit — change the rhythm, target, or tracking type. Past days keep the rules that were active when you logged them; only new days use the update.
  • Pause — for a stretch where the goal genuinely doesn't apply (illness, travel). Paused days show no color at all and never count toward streaks or stats. Resume whenever you're ready.
  • Archive — retire a goal for good. It disappears from Today and Calendar, but its full history stays on Goal Detail if you ever want to look back.

Filtering your view

The chip row above every calendar screen — All, then one chip per category, then one per individual goal — narrows everything below it to just what you tap. Handy when one crowded day is hard to read at a glance.

Settings & backup

Week starts on

Monday or Sunday — changes the first column of every calendar and week view immediately.

Reminders

One daily notification time, listing whatever's still open that day.

Export data

Saves every goal, log, and setting to a single file — a backup, or a way to move to a new device.

Import data

Restores from an exported file; Tracker warns you first about anything that would conflict.

Quick answers

Why does my goal still show gray after I marked it done?

Check which screen you're on. A weekly or monthly goal's rollup stays gray until the whole period is decided, even though that day's own checkmark on Today/Day View already went green — see Today vs. the whole period.

I undid a past day and it turned red instead of gray — is that a bug?

No — a day that's already over only has two honest outcomes: done, or missed. Gray only ever means "still undecided," which is only true for today or the future. Undoing a past day correctly falls back to red if nothing was logged.

What's the difference between At Least, At Most, and Exactly?

At Least is a floor (hit it or go over). At Most is a ceiling (stay at or under it). Exactly demands the precise number — going over fails it immediately, same as At Most.

Can I log a day I missed a week ago?

Yes. Open that date from the Calendar and log it exactly as you would today — Tracker treats every date the same way for logging.

Does pausing a goal hurt my streak?

No. A paused stretch is skipped entirely — it's neither a success nor a failure, and your streak picks up again unaffected once you resume.
